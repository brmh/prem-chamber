An affidavit is a statement of facts, made on oath, signed by the person making it and attested by someone authorised to administer oaths. That is all it is. It is not a permission, a certificate or a court order, and it does not make something true.

This matters because affidavits are usually asked for in situations where people assume the document itself solves the problem. It does not. It puts a person on oath about a fact, and attaches a consequence if that statement is false.

## The three things an affidavit must get right

**The deponent.** Who is swearing, with full name, parentage, age and address, matching the identity proof.

**The facts.** Stated in numbered paragraphs, in the first person, each one a fact and not an argument. Anything not within the deponent's personal knowledge is stated as being believed to be true on information received, and the source is named.

**The verification.** The clause at the end stating which paragraphs are true to personal knowledge and which are believed to be true. An affidavit without a proper verification clause is the one that comes back.

## Why offices reject affidavits

Almost never for the stamp value. Nearly always for the wording.

The office asking for the affidavit usually has a specific thing it wants stated, in a specific way, and that expectation is not written on the request letter. A generic affidavit downloaded from the internet says something adjacent to what is needed, and gets returned.

The fix is simple: bring the letter, form or notice that asked for the affidavit. The draft is then matched to it.

## The affidavits people actually need

**Name change or correction.** Where the name differs between documents, or where a name has been changed. This affidavit usually accompanies a newspaper publication and, where applicable, a gazette notification. The affidavit alone does not change your name in any record; it supports the applications that do.

**Date of birth correction.** Where the school record, Aadhaar and other documents disagree. The affidavit explains the discrepancy; the correction itself is made by the authority holding the record.

**Same person.** Stating that two differently spelt names in different documents refer to one person. This is the quiet workhorse of Indian paperwork.

**Income, residence and dependency.** For scholarships, admissions and various schemes.

**Lost document.** For a duplicate mark sheet, registration certificate or policy. Usually paired with a police report.

**Gap in education or employment.** Stating what the deponent was doing during a period not covered by certificates.

**Single status.** Often needed for a marriage abroad, or for a visa. These usually need attestation beyond an Oath Commissioner, so check the requirement of the country concerned before getting one made.

## What to bring

- Your identity proof, in original
- Any document the affidavit refers to, such as the school certificate or Aadhaar
- The letter or form from the office that asked for the affidavit, where there is one
- Stamp paper of the correct value, which can also be arranged at the chamber

The deponent has to attend personally, because the oath is administered to them. Most affidavits are drafted and attested within the hour.

## One caution

An affidavit is sworn. A false statement in one is not merely a paperwork problem; it carries consequences under the law. Read what is written before signing it, and do not sign a statement about facts you are not sure of because someone at a counter said it was routine.
