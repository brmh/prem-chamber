A power of attorney is an authority to act for someone else. It is useful, ordinary, and routinely misused in property transactions in this region.

## General and special

A **special power of attorney** authorises one defined act: to appear and execute a sale deed for one particular property, to collect a specific document, to appear in one proceeding. It ends when that act is done.

A **general power of attorney** authorises a class of acts over time: managing property, dealing with banks, appearing before authorities.

Almost every problem with powers of attorney comes from a general power drafted in the widest possible terms, handed to someone trusted, and then forgotten about. Keep the authority as narrow as the job requires. A special power is the right instrument far more often than people assume.

## Why property cannot be bought on a GPA

This is worth stating plainly because it continues to cost people their savings.

A sale of immovable property is effected by a registered sale deed. A power of attorney authorises an agent to act; it does not convey ownership to the agent.

The Supreme Court has held that transactions in the form of a general power of attorney, an agreement to sell and a will do not convey title. Someone buying "on GPA" does not become the owner. They hold a power that can be revoked, that dies with the person who gave it, and that gives them nothing to register in their own name later.

When property is offered only on a GPA, there is always a reason: an incomplete chain of title, a freehold conversion never done, agricultural land that cannot be transferred to the buyer, or unpaid dues. That reason does not disappear when the money changes hands. It becomes yours.

## Executing one from abroad

For someone outside India, the usual routes are:

- signing before the Indian Embassy or High Commission in that country; or
- signing before a local notary and having the document apostilled, where that country is party to the Hague Convention.

After the document reaches India it has to be stamped within the prescribed period. Only then can it be acted on. Plan for the courier time, because the person waiting in India cannot do anything until the original arrives.

## Registration

A power of attorney authorising the sale of immovable property must be registered. Others may be notarised, depending on the purpose and on what the receiving office insists on. Banks, authorities and registry offices each have their own requirements, so find out what the office in question will accept before the document is drawn.

## Drafting it safely

- **List the acts.** Not "to do all acts necessary", but each act the attorney may perform.
- **Identify the property.** Where it relates to property, describe that property and no other.
- **Put a time limit in it.** A power that expires on a stated date cannot be used a decade later.
- **Exclude what should be excluded.** Say expressly that the attorney may not sell, mortgage or gift, where that is the intention.
- **Keep the original safe.** Give a certified copy where one will do.

## Revoking one

A power of attorney does not lapse because the work finished. Until it is revoked it can be used.

Revocation should be in writing, and notice of it should be given to the attorney and to anyone likely to deal with them — the bank, the authority, the registry office. Where the power was registered, the revocation should be registered too. A power of attorney forgotten in a drawer for years is a live authority in someone else's hands.
