A marriage certificate is one of those documents nobody needs until suddenly everybody needs it at once: a passport application, a visa, a bank nomination, adding a spouse to a service record, changing a name. Then the question becomes how to produce one for a wedding that happened years ago.

## Registration is not the marriage

It is worth separating two things that get confused constantly.

**Registration** records a marriage that has already taken place, whether last month or twenty years ago. It produces a certificate.

**Court marriage** under the Special Marriage Act is the marriage itself, solemnised before a Marriage Officer after a thirty-day notice. That is a different process, covered in the [court marriage guide](/guides/court-marriage-ghaziabad).

If you are already married, you want registration.

## What is needed

- Proof of date of birth for both spouses: school certificate, Aadhaar, passport or birth certificate
- Proof of address for both
- Proof that the marriage took place: wedding photographs, the invitation card, a certificate from the temple, gurudwara or church, or the priest's declaration
- Aadhaar and photographs of two witnesses who were present at the wedding
- A joint photograph of the couple
- Where either spouse was married before: the decree of divorce, or the death certificate of the former spouse

## The steps

1. **The application is filed on the state portal.** Details of both spouses, the date and place of the marriage, and the witnesses are entered, documents are uploaded and the fee is paid.

2. **Affidavits are prepared.** A joint affidavit of the couple stating the fact and date of the marriage, and declarations by the witnesses, are drawn up and attested.

3. **An appointment is given.** The couple and both witnesses attend the Sub-Registrar office on that date with the originals.

4. **The certificate is issued.** Where the papers are in order it is usually issued the same day.

## Older marriages, and what changes

For a wedding a few months old this is straightforward. The difficulty comes with marriages from ten or twenty years ago, where nobody kept the card and the photographs are gone.

What carries weight then:

- the witness statements, which become the main evidence rather than a formality;
- documents from that period showing the couple living as husband and wife, such as a ration card, a joint bank account, a service record naming the spouse, or the children's school records naming both parents;
- any temple or community register entry from the time.

It is worth gathering these before applying rather than after an objection is raised.

## Where applications get stuck

**Name mismatches.** The name on the school certificate, the Aadhaar and the wedding card frequently differ. This is the commonest reason for an application being held up, and it is usually resolved by an affidavit explaining that the names refer to the same person.

**Date disputes.** Where the card shows one date and the photographs suggest another, the discrepancy has to be explained rather than ignored.

**Witnesses who were not actually there.** A witness who cannot answer basic questions about the wedding creates a problem in the one document you cannot afford to have questioned later.

**Incomplete proof of a previous marriage ending.** Where a spouse was married before, the divorce decree or death certificate is not optional.

## A note on timing

Where the certificate is needed for a passport or a visa appointment, start early. For a recent marriage with complete papers, one to two weeks from application to certificate is normal. For an old marriage with thin evidence, allow considerably more, because the office may ask for additional material before it is satisfied.
