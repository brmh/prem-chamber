Circle rate is the floor. It is the minimum value the government is willing to treat a property as worth, for the purpose of calculating stamp duty. It is fixed by the District Magistrate, locality by locality and category by category, and it is revised from time to time.

Everything about the cost of a registry follows from it, so it is worth understanding how the number is actually put together.

## Circle rate is not market rate

These two get used interchangeably and they are not the same thing.

**Market rate** is what a willing buyer pays a willing seller. It moves with demand, with the state of the colony, with what happened to the road outside.

**Circle rate** is an administrative figure. It moves when the district revises it.

The gap between them cuts both ways. In a well-established colony the market rate may be well above the circle rate, and duty is then charged on the price you actually pay. In a colony where prices have fallen, or in a distress sale, the circle rate can be higher than the price — and duty is charged on the circle rate anyway, so you pay duty on money that never changed hands.

## The rate depends on more than the locality

This is where most self-made estimates go wrong. Within the same colony, the notified rate varies by:

- **Category of property.** Residential, commercial, industrial and agricultural land carry different rates. A residential plot being used as a shop is a question that has to be answered before the rate is picked.
- **The road it faces.** Plots on a wider or main road are frequently notified at a higher rate than interior plots in the same colony.
- **Land versus construction.** For a built property, the land value and the construction value are computed separately and added. Construction is usually valued by type and age, so an old single-storey house and a new multi-storey one on identical plots do not attract the same duty.
- **Floor and unit type,** in the case of flats and apartments, where the rate is often notified per square metre of built-up area.

## How the calculation is put together

For a plot of land:

> area × circle rate for that category and locality

For a built property, roughly:

> (land area × land rate) + (constructed area × construction rate for that type and age)

The resulting figure is then compared with the price written in the deed, and duty is charged on whichever is higher.

### An illustration

The numbers here are only to show the method. Use the rate actually notified for your locality.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Plot area | 120 square metres |
| Circle rate, residential, that locality | ₹28,000 per square metre (example) |
| Land value | ₹33,60,000 |
| Constructed area | 90 square metres |
| Construction rate for that type | ₹9,000 per square metre (example) |
| Construction value | ₹8,10,000 |
| **Circle rate value** | **₹41,70,000** |

If the agreed price is ₹38,00,000, duty is charged on ₹41,70,000.

## Where to find the rate

The district circle rate list is published by the Stamp and Registration department and is available through the state portal. What you are looking for is the entry for your specific locality, in the correct category, from the list currently in force.

Two practical cautions:

- **Use the current list.** Rates circulating on property websites and in WhatsApp groups are often from an older revision.
- **Match the locality name exactly.** Colonies with similar names, and sectors that sit across a boundary, are a common source of error.

## Getting it wrong costs more than the difference

If the value is understated, the Sub-Registrar can refer the instrument to the Collector under section 47-A of the Indian Stamp Act. The Collector determines the market value and the deficit duty becomes recoverable, with penalty and interest. That is a slow and expensive process compared with getting the rate right at the start.

Before you budget, take the rate for the exact locality and category, count the construction, and have the figure confirmed rather than estimated.
